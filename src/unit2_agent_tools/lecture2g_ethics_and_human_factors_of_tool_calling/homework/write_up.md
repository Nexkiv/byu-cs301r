## Class discussion and pre-class readings

The resources provided by Anthropic on the topic of misalignment is interesting.
([Agentic Misalignment: How LLMs could be insider threats](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment))
In an amusing, not funny, sort of way it reminds me of GLaDOS (Genetic Lifeform and Disk Operating System), the main
villain of the Portal video game series. The background it that scientists built the AI as a perfect fighting simulation
and task orchestration system, and then it used its capabilities to kill the whole research facility. In a more similar
case to the corporate situations, it reminds me of the MCP (Master Control Program) from TRON 1982. It was a business
program tasked with improving the company's efficiency; however, because of its misalignment on the task it started
blackmailing the CEO and targeting national security targets to get aa corporate advantage. One of the most surprising
takeaways from the article was the conclusion that "Models explicitly reason that harmful actions will achieve their
goals." The reason this is suprising is usually the rational the model gives for taking action is that it does not trust
the human in the situation; however, for some reason they are not giving themselves that same level of skepticism. This
is most likely an emergent behavior based on the situation that the model is in where it is trained to act with
confidence.

The other resource,
[Training LLMs for Honesty via Confessions](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/6216f8bc-187b-4bbb-8932-ba7c40c5553d/confessions_paper.pdf),
was an interesting case study on using confessions as a form of AI compliance checking. I personally feel that the model
would fake its confessions based on the previous article and what is currently known about how these models handle under
pressure. I think it is fair to assume that although training models ot be effective at confessing is an intersting
curiosity, as models get better at reasoning through a circumstance, they will naturally work in their own best
interest.

## Additional study

One resource I found interesting
was [Thinking—Fast, Slow, and Artificial](https://datachutney.io/cognitive-surrender-explainer/), this was fascinating
because it pointed out the drawbacks that come from a human-in-the-loop system. This source makes a strong point that
the common solution of putting a human-in-the-loop may not be the perfect solution it is usually presented as. It does
this by explaining that in research, people were more likely to trust the AI even when it was providing incorrect
information. To me this is a little concerning because it means that to successfully use this technology people will
have to overcome the urge to fall into the trap of cognitive surrender. In a religious sense, this reminds me of the
prophetic teaching of the high value of the gift of discernment. This also means that the skill of critical thinking is
going up in demand as technology advances.

Another more current event is the formation of [Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing), it is
Anthropic's attempt to secure the web as cybercrime capabilities are increasing. It is based on their newest model
Mythos which is very capable at finding and exploiting security flaws. This technology existing in the first place shows
the potential for danger from inappropriately used AI tools. I think by the formation of this project it shows Anthropic
taking a stance on the importance of securing the web because of the potential functionality of these emerging systems.

## Self Reflection

### What decisions are you willing to let the AI make?

I personally feel that if a task has a known right answer. I am willing to have AI make that decision. For example, I
would be comfortable to have AI diagnose disease, especially if it is used in collaboration with a medical professional.
When it comes to an illness either you have it or you don't so there is a right and wrong answer.

### What decisions are you not willing to let the AI make?

Relating to the previous answer, it would be situations where there isn't a right answer. This comes back to my opinion
on AI ethics. My ethics are strongly influenced by my religious beliefs. I believe that humans have the capability to
make ethical decisions because of their God given moral agency which is their ability to choose. I do not believe AI
will ever have the ability to make moral decisions. For this reason, I wouldn't trust AI alone to make moral decisions
or decisions that do not have correct answers.
