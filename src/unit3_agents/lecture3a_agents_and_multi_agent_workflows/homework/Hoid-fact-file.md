# Hoid — Canonical Fact File (Brandon Sanderson’s Cosmere)

Brief: a concise dossier of canonical facts, scene-style examples, and practical guidance for including the actual Cosmere character Hoid as a background cameo in a new story. All statements are restricted to published canon (novels/novellas) and Brandon Sanderson’s public Word‑of‑Brandon / interviews; I note when a point comes from on‑page text vs. author commentary.

---

## 1) Quick overview
- Identity / aliases: The same recurring person across the Cosmere, usually called Hoid (also appears under many aliases: Wit, Cephandrius, Topaz, Drifter, etc.) (canonical; appears across multiple books).
- Role in the books: A long‑lived world‑hopper who shows up as a storyteller/jester/beggar/informant; he often delivers tone, tiny lore beats, or small plot nudges without taking center stage.
- Canonical sources (selected): Elantris; Warbreaker; Mistborn (including Mistborn: Secret History); The Stormlight Archive (Way of Kings, Words of Radiance, Oathbringer, Rhythm of War, plus related novellas such as Edgedancer); White Sand; Tress of the Emerald Sea; plus Brandon Sanderson’s FAQ and WoB statements (Arcanum/WoB). (See Sources section for specifics.)

## 2) Canonical biography & timeline (what is confirmed)
- Born on Yolen prior to the Shattering (canonical via author statements / WoB).
- Present at the Shattering of Adonalsium; offered a Shard and refused it (Word‑of‑Brandon / interviews).
- At one point he briefly possessed a Dawnshard; this changed his spiritweb and left lasting consequences (WoB / author commentary).
- He traveled widely afterward and is the same person who appears (in disguise or named) in many Cosmere books.
- On Scadrial (events in Mistborn: Secret History) he entered the Well/Perpendicularity and stole a bead of lerasium and consumed it (this is shown in the novella Mistborn: Secret History).
- He continues to be active across worlds; many early details and full chronology remain deliberately unrevealed (author RAFOs on full early life / Dragonsteel details).

Degree of certainty: the above bullets are canonical; items labeled as WoB are author statements rather than being fully spelled out in a single novel.

## 3) Motives / goals (canonical hints)
- Broadly summarized by Brandon Sanderson: Hoid’s long‑term aim has been hinted as “to make that which once was” (WoB). This is intentionally cryptic.
- On‑page behavior consistent with that hint: he seeks old knowledge and artifacts, shows interest in Investiture and major events, and inserts himself where ancient powers or important turning points occur.
- Explicit full goals and endpoint: not confirmed in canon; many specifics remain purposefully unrevealed by the author (RAFO).

Uncertainty: the phrase above is a WoB hint—useful for tone and motivation but not a full mission statement.

## 4) Abilities & Investiture (what’s shown vs. what’s WoB)
What is shown on‑page (directly in novels/novellas)
- Worldhopping: moves between planets via the Cognitive Realm/Shadesmar and Perpendicularities (shown/strongly implied across Stormlight Archive and Mistborn: Secret History).
- Mistborn powers (Allomancy): he steals and consumes lerasium in Mistborn: Secret History; this grants him Allomantic capability (on‑page events in that novella).
- Cognitive‑realm combat: he fights and interacts with Kelsier in the Cognitive Realm in Mistborn: Secret History (on‑page).
- Storytelling illusions/spectacles: in Warbreaker (chapter 32) he performs a sand/smoke storytelling display; on Roshar he acts as the King’s Wit and produces Lightweaving‑like effects visually/aurally in performance scenes (on‑page, with Brandon’s annotations linking technique to Yolish Lightweaving).
- Use of Breaths/Awakening: he is depicted/use is implied historically to have used BioChromatic Breath and Awakening effects (WoB plus in‑book cues in Warbreaker era scenes); specifics about current Breath holdings are not fully detailed on‑page.

What is derived from Words of Brandon (WoB) / author commentary
- He was present at the Shattering and explicitly refused a Shard (WoB).
- He once temporarily held a Dawnshard and was changed by it (WoB / author statements).
- He has collected various forms of Investiture over time (WoB); the full list and mechanics are not exhaustively documented on‑page.
- Brandon has confirmed Hoid is not Adonalsium and is not a Shardholder (WoB).

Limits and cautions
- Many of Hoid’s higher‑level capabilities are suggested rather than exhaustively shown; do not assume omnipotence. Several mechanics (exact limits of his Dawnshard effects, full power set) are only clarified in WoBs and remain partly ambiguous in the books.

## 5) Personality, mannerisms & signature traits (how he reads on the page)
- Tone: wry, teasing, often performative humor that hides seriousness. He’ll insult or mock to provoke thought.
- Occupation/style: storyteller / jester / King’s Wit / beggar / coachman — the role changes to suit the scene.
- Props & style: uses simple props (colored sand/dust, small trinkets, a distinctive flute in some Rosharan interludes), short theatrical vignettes, and sometimes music. He layers theatrical performance over subtle information delivery.
- Speech: aphoristic, pithy, and sometimes cryptic; a habit of dropping one‑line observational zingers.
- Behavior in scenes: rarely tries to take over the protagonists’ arcs; he acts to prompt, hint, hand off an object, or catalyze a thought, then departs.

Canonical feel: see Warbreaker (storyteller sand scene); Stormlight Archive (Wit court scenes); Mistborn (Secret History) for his sparring and more serious side.

## 6) Notable canonical appearances (short scene descriptions you can model)
(If you want chapter‑by‑chapter citations I can compile them; below I list representative on‑page cameos with scene descriptions.)

- Elantris — cameo as a beggar/anonymous helper: appears in a way that advances a plot beat while staying discreet; he helps Sarene in small, functional ways (book cameo).
  - How to model: a disguised helper who performs one practical deed (smuggle an item, pass a message) then slips away.

- Warbreaker — explicit storyteller scene (chapter 32): introduced by Lightsong; Hoid performs a colorful sand/dust storytelling display, dramatizing history while entertaining and giving subtextual information.
  - How to model: an entertainer at court or tavern who tells a two‑minute story using small props and leaves the audience with a pointed, slightly uncomfortable truth.

- Mistborn (The Final Empire — cameo) and Mistborn: Secret History — larger role in the Cognitive Realm: in Era 1/Secret History he interacts with Kelsier, steals/consumes lerasium, and fights in the Cognitive Realm; Secret History reveals significant, direct involvement in Scadrial’s events.
  - How to model: a seemingly marginal figure who gives a small object or provokes a private confrontation that foreshadows larger forces.

- Stormlight Archive — multiple Wit appearances (The Way of Kings and later books): shows up as the King’s Wit/performer, trades barbed jests with highborn characters, tells short stories, sometimes performs small Lightweaves/illusions.
  - How to model: a court fool who uses humor to ask dangerous questions and gives one cryptic line that makes the protagonist pause.

- Tress of the Emerald Sea / other recent Cosmere: Hoid appears and is referenced across newer Cosmere works—useful to read these for updated tone and behaviors.

General cameo pattern (Sanderson’s model)
1) Inconspicuous role tied to the setting (bard, beggar, coachman).
2) Single small action (tell a short story, hand over an item, create a brief illusion).
3) Signature flavor detail (sand, a certain turn of phrase, a flute, or a wink).

## 7) Practical guidance for your friend — how to include Hoid believably as a background character
Do:
- Keep his appearance brief and scene‑appropriate. Use the 1‑action rule: he does one useful thing (gives an object, asks a question, tells a short anecdote).
- Pick a plausible role for the locale: traveling storyteller in an inn; beggar in a city market; court Wit at a feast; coachman at a crossroads.
- Give one signature detail to identify him (e.g., colored sand, a small carved topaz, a wry one‑liner such as “We all think ourselves clever until the world reminds us how small our cleverness is”).
- Keep him from hijacking the protagonist’s story. His cameo should add atmosphere, a beat of foreshadowing, or a tiny plot nudge.
- If you want a “cosmic” hint, let him drop an enigmatic line (WoB style) rather than explicit exposition.

Don’t:
- Don’t let him solve the main conflict or display world‑changing powers in a background cameo. He can do a startling small thing (a brief illusion, handing over a trinket, subtly influencing feelings) but major interventions feel out of character for cameo use.
- Avoid long infodumps. Sanderson’s usage is brief, theatrical, and enigmatic.

Short templates your friend can lift and adapt
- Inn Telling: Hoid sits near the hearth, scatters a pinch of bright sand, tells a two‑sentence parable about kings who forgot their names, then slips a folded scrap to the protagonist and leaves before it’s read.
- Market Beggar: a ragged figure bumps a traveler, murmurs “You look like you could use a memory kept safe,” and presses a tiny sealed phial into their hand—then vanishes into the crowd.
- Court Wit: during a banquet, the Wit rises to recite a mocking couplet that disguises a suggestion about a forgotten treaty; nobles laugh, but one character looks unsettled.

These mirror canonical cameo mechanics (see Warbreaker, Elantris, Stormlight Archive).

## 8) Notable uncertainties & things to avoid assuming
- Full early backstory and complete list of powers are intentionally unrevealed; many details exist only as WoB hints or will be filled in future works (Dragonsteel is the planned reveal path). Treat anything beyond shown on‑page and explicit WoBs as speculative.
- The precise current inventory and limits of his Investiture (what he can do in any given scene) are not exhaustively defined on‑page—avoid making him a deus ex machina unless you frame it as an explicit, story‑level reveal.
- Some claims (like the Dawnshard possession and its full effects) are based primarily on author statements rather than detailed scenes; flag those as WoB when discussing them.

---

## 9) Quick writing checklist (for including Hoid)
- Role fits the scene? (beggar, entertainer, coachman, Wit)
- One small action only? (object, line, short story)
- Signature prop/phrase present? (sand, flute, topaz, one‑liner)
- No major plot hijack? (keeps cameo peripheral)
- Tone: playful / cryptic, with an undertow of seriousness
