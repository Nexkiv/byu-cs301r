## Part 1: Good Usage

One way I feel that is a good usage of AI is the enabling of creative thinkers to achieve more of what they have in
their mind.
[This article]( https://mynintendonews.com/2025/12/26/level-5-ceo-responds-to-viral-post-about-companys-use-of-ai/)
from Level 5, a video game company, describes their approach. I particularly like his opinion on the moral neutrality of
AI. He is describing it as a tool that can help developers, “to enrich the creative world.” It also reminds me of what
Eddie Drake, CTO of Disney’s live action special effects division, said in a presentation to the animation majors. He
described that at Disney they are using AI to enable artists not to replace them. He then described some key past
projects where AI has helped them express ideas and accomplish tasks more effectively.

## Part 2: Bad Usage

I think the current government situation with its intended deal with Anthropic and OpenAI is a poor use of AI.
[This](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/27/nx-s1-5729118/trump-anthropic-pentagon-openai-ai-weapons-ban) was an article
posted recently on the subject in response to the US government's decision to ban the use of Anthropic's software. I can
see the government's intent to use AI "**for all lawful purposes**." As a matter of national security it makes sense to
leverage this tool to accelerate the advancement of threat prevention and response effectiveness. Despite this, I feel
that Anthropics opinion that, "**we do not believe that today's frontier AI models are reliable enough** to be used in
fully
autonomous weapons. Allowing current models to be used in this way would endanger America's warfighters and civilians."
This is a valid point. We currently do not have a good amount of data to say that the kind of system that the government
would want to set up would even work safely. Anthropic also points out the moral problems with mass surveillance and
autonomous weapons systems. The reason why I think this is a poor use of AI is because it is in an untested area of the
field and this kind of use and would violate the personal rights of those who the technology is being used on.

## Part 3: Project Update

I made some real headway. I migrated my project from Gradio to Flask + Tailwind CSS + HTMX because that is the
recommended UI change from Gradio. The process I used was an iterative one. I made the migration then I gave a
web-extension llm assistant access to the page and told it to conduct a full UI/UX review. I then reviewed its output
and passed it back to Claude code. I repeated this process several times. I also worked on keeping the scope of the
project established without succumbing to scope creep. The website final presentation looks more visually appealing, and
it can do many of the visual things that Gradio was incapable of. That is something that I learned throughout this
process. When building a project certain frameworks are more capable at accomplishing tasks than others. Perhaps it
would have been possible to build my web-app in Gradio, but by transferring to a framework specific for the task I am
setting myself up for continued success.
