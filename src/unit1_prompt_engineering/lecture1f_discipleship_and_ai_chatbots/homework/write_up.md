## Things as the Really Are 2.0

*Elder David A. Bednar*

One thing that I personally really appreciated in Elder Bednar's address was his statement that "neither digital
innovations nor rapid change in and of themselves are good or evil." I feel like most of the people I interact with on a
daily basis are quick to explain how technological innovations will be the death of us all. It is comforting to hear an
apostle explain that the Lord has a plan and that it is our responsibility to use these innovations in our day for good.

I find it particularly poignant that Elder Bednar cautioned against the dangers of AI companions. Especially because he
isn't painting all technology with a broad brush in this address, but pointing out a specific danger. To me, this is
very important. Elder Bednar is pointing out a danger in our day and age that we need to root out. It almost feels like
Joseph Smith's declaration in the 89th section of the Doctrine and Covenants where he states that the purpose of the
Word of Wisdom is to combat the "evils and designs which do and will exist in the hearts of conspiring men in the last
days" (D&C 89:4).

Elder Bednar describes four ways that AI can be used for good, "to learn, to communicate, to lift and brighten lives,
and to build and strengthen the Church." I think I need to allocate more time of my personal study to these areas and
how I, as an agentic engineer, can help to further the Lord's work in these areas.

I like the way that Elder Bednar described the thinking capabilities of AI as being only able to "think telestial." It
creates an accurate gap between the invitation of President Nelson to "think celestial." Along these lines, my favorite
paragraph of his address is this one:

"Truth is knowledge of things as they really are. Artificial intelligence cannot simulate, imitate, or replace the
influence of the Holy Ghost in our lives. No matter how sophisticated and elegant AI technology ultimately may become,
it simply can never bear witness of the Father and the Son, reveal the truth of all things, or sanctify those who have
repented and been baptized."

This concept is also discussed by Elder Gong in his address from the Rome Summit on Ethics. (I will talk more about it
below.)

Another warning in Elder Bednar's remarks is on the subject of the danger of losing our desire to work and to learn. I
personally have taken this as a challenge, and so I work hard to make sure that I am learning especially when I am using
AI tools.

Another take away that I got from Elder Bednar was his teaching that AI should not be used to generate a rough draft or
a final version of whatever it is we are making. That process should be one where we are able to act upon the promptings
of the Spirit and follow His direction.

## Faith, Respect, and Moral Compass in an Age of Artificial Intelligence

*Elder Gerrit W. Gong*

I like Elder Gongs phrasing of, "I noted we do not fear AI, nor do we think AI is the answer to everything." I feel like
this viewpoint is the one that will lead to the most effective discussion about AI usage in the future. That is why I
think a balanced approach is the most effective at fostering innovation, but still being weary of dangers.

Elder Gong uses the phrasing that we as children of God are "animated by God-given moral agency", another way that I
usually rephrase this is as its antithesis, "Artificial Intelligence is incapable of acting upon the promptings of the
Spirit." This is in line with what Elder Bednar was teaching in his remarks about the limitations of AI and how as
members of the church we need to be vigilant in our own personal learning and in our connection with God.

Going along with that concept, a little later in the address, Elder Gong stated, "our human capacity to pause, hesitate,
even doubt opens us to serendipity, grace, inspiration." I think this gets at a key part of the technology. It is our
responsibility to use it with a mind open to the inspiration og God. If we think it can replace that, we are sorely
mistaken. However, as we embrace innovation with an eye single to the glory of God we can do truly miraculous things.

## Conclusion

I think AI chatbots are effective at brainstorming and, when given web access, research. For this reason I think that
they should be used in those circumstances. On the other hand, I think that AI chatbots would be very effective at
establishing an emotional connection with a user. I personally do not see this as a good thing. In lines with the
warnings given by Elder Bednar, I think that the use of AI for connection is a danger an inappropriate use of AI chatbot
technology.
